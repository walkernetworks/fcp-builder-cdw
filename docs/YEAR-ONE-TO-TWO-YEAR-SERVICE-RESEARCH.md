# Year-One-to-Two-Year Follow-On Service Research

## Purpose

This note captures the external guidance used to shape a **non-committal** two-year follow-on outlook. It is a planning aid, not a statement that any advanced service is licensed, deployed, or in contractual scope.

## Authoritative service themes

VMware Cloud Foundation 9.1 describes VCF Automation and VCF Operations as modern interfaces for application provisioning that sit above the private-cloud foundation. It positions the platform around operational consistency, resilience, security, and support for production AI.[1]

VMware Cloud Foundation Operations identifies follow-on operational themes that logically build on an established platform: fleet configuration and drift management, proactive health and diagnostics, capacity and cost management, lifecycle management, security operations, and third-party visibility integrations.[2]

VMware Cloud Foundation Automation describes follow-on service areas that should be considered only when confirmed by the customer: self-service IaaS, blueprints and infrastructure as code, governance and policy-as-code, tenant and content management, Kubernetes multi-cluster management, network and security automation, orchestration and extensibility, and private-AI services.[3]

## Design implications

The operational year-one plan should establish the foundation and initial adoption outcomes. The two-year notional outlook should then begin after that horizon and contain only dependencies that follow from the completed year-one work and selected detailed-intake priorities. Each outlook activity should make its enabling year-one service explicit and be labeled as a strategic consideration rather than a delivery commitment.
