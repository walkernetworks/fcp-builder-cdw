Summary

CDW designs and builds a new VCF 9.1 foundation for Acme Health on new hardware, proves the migration runbook on the first waves, and hands the platform and the runbook to Acme Health's team, who run the remaining waves. VCF Operations becomes the team's daily view of health and capacity. Recovery design and the first workload domain follow after the entitlement.

Situation

  • A DR site is being built: 8 new hosts and a NetApp array fed by SnapMirror. Recovery time objective and orchestration are not yet defined.
  • Recovery of 330 powered-on VMs is manual today.
  • The team wants to "make our lives easier" and use features it has already paid for.

Plan on a page

  • Program management: kickoff and delivery plan, hardware compatibility review. Assessment and discovery are already delivered.
  • Infrastructure: design and bill of materials, VCF foundation build, migration plan, proving waves.
  • Operations: VCF Operations design, role and access mapping, licensing, training, handover, runbook transfer.
  • Cloud services: not requested (Kubernetes, Private AI, HCX).
  • Security and resilience: recovery design and build, after the entitlement.

VCF design profile

ConsumptionVCF 9.1 with VCF Operations as the daily front door; vCenter consumption unchanged for the team; automation evaluated later.
Physical sitePrimary data center and a new DR site.
AvailabilityConverged management domain on the primary cluster; HA and fully automated DRS retained.
IsolationNSX deployed with VCF, VLAN-backed at first; no network redesign on day one.
RecoverabilityDR site as a separate workload domain in the same fleet; orchestration tool chosen in the recovery design.
ExpansionEvacuated hosts rebuilt into VCF wave by wave; the DR workload domain added once the site is online.
