How hours are built

The plan picks catalog lines from the pathway, the estate and the outcomes. Each line has a base size in lead-role hours. The other roles are added as fixed shares, then the hours are spread across the timeline stages that deliver that line, weighted by stage length.

lead role = base hours
other technical role = 15% × base (X16 uses 20%; DX uses 0)
project manager = 25% × (architect + consultant) (DX uses 0)
loaded line ≈ 1.4375 × base

What drives the bracket

  • Hosts come from RVTools (or the brief's ESX host count). They pick the design (A), discovery (DD), deploy (D) and VVF upgrade (U) brackets.
  • Sites are data centers from RVTools or the brief. More than one site moves design and discovery up a bracket.
  • VCF instances (brief, default 1) and workload domains (0 when consolidated, otherwise 1) pick the deploy bracket.
  • Consolidated topology is chosen, or assumed when it's undecided and there are 10 hosts or fewer. Only D1 needs it.
  • vCenters (brief) set how many import lines (X1) the import pathway carries.
  • Prior assessment delivered removes the discovery lines (DD and DX). Requirements then share the architecture hours.
  • Outcomes add the X lines. On design-only and undecided pathways each add-on keeps 25% of its base, which is its design share.

Where the lines land

  • DD and DX go on the Requirements stage. A goes on the other design stages, and on Requirements too when there's a prior assessment.
  • D and U go on the build stages, split by weight: management domain 1, workload domain 0.8, VVF cluster 0.4, preparation/validation 0.4, handover 0.2, licensing 0.1.
  • Wave lines (FILL) go on the execution stages. X lines go on their implementation stages, or on the component design stage when only the design is in scope.
  • Kickoff, approval and design authority stages carry only the design phase's project-management hours.

What to research

These are the places where the model doesn't move with the estate, or moves in big steps. They're the best places to check against real engagements.

Migration and upgrade waves don't scale with hosts or VMs. Without an entitlement they're fixed at 60 base hours (migration) or 40 (in-place), which is 86 or 57 loaded, whether the estate has 8 hosts or 150. With an entitlement they grow to fill whatever is left of it. Research hours per wave, per 100 VMs or per cluster.
Add-ons are flat. Every X line has the same hours at 10 hosts and at 150, at one site and at four. NSX, vDefend and Live Recovery probably scale with clusters, sites or protected VMs.
Depth tiers don't change hours. Tier 1 (deployed), Tier 2 (operationalized) and Tier 3 (business consumption) are recorded and shown, but they price the same. Efficiency is the only area where depth matters, and only as selected or not.
Discovery jumps at 51 hosts. Requirements goes from about 46 hours to about 116 when the estate passes 50 hosts at one site (DD1 34 → DD2 85). At more than 100 hosts or more than 3 sites it's about 273 (DD3 200).
Deploy jumps at 25 hosts. D2 covers up to 24 hosts, so a 25-host greenfield moves to D4 (135 → 210 base). The foundation build goes from 92 to 144 hours, and the first workload domain from 74 to 115. Then it's flat until 125 hosts.
In-place upgrades price the "bring under VCF management" step like a full deploy. It uses the same D bracket as a greenfield build: 178 hours at 25–100 hosts and 346 at 150.
Imports are priced per vCenter, not per host. X1 is 70 base for each vCenter in the brief, with no change for cluster or host count.
Discovery per area is 8 architect hours, with no PM. Each area of interest adds the same 8 hours whatever the estate size.
Storage choice doesn't change hours. vSAN, NFS, FC and other existing storage price the same.
